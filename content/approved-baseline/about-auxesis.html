About Auxesis

Why Auxesis exists.

I founded Auxesis Education to help students make progress in science and become more capable, rather than more dependent on tutoring. I support their academic goals while helping them tackle the challenges in front of them.

The name

Auxesis means growth.

Education is, at its heart, about growth. Not simply acquiring knowledge or achieving a particular grade, but developing the understanding, skills, confidence and independence to keep learning.

Growth looks different for every student. For one, it might mean building the foundations needed to move from struggling to succeeding. For another, it might mean moving from meeting expectations to exceeding them. Sometimes the most important progress is less immediately visible: asking a better question, making a connection between ideas, challenging an assumption or approaching an unfamiliar problem with confidence.

At Auxesis Education, I believe learning should be active, purposeful and increasingly independent. Students should not simply memorise scientific facts or follow procedures without understanding why they work. They should learn to think critically, explore evidence, recognise patterns, apply knowledge in new situations and develop the confidence to question what they encounter.

That philosophy shapes how I facilitate learning. Rather than simply providing answers or delivering information, I create opportunities for students to question, investigate, make connections and construct their own understanding. I meet learners where they are, providing appropriate guidance, challenge and support while gradually encouraging greater independence. The aim is not to remove every difficulty, but to help students develop the confidence, curiosity and skills to navigate challenges themselves.

Growth is not always linear, and it cannot always be measured by a grade. Progress can involve uncertainty, mistakes, revisiting old ideas and discovering new ways of thinking. These experiences are not obstacles to learning; they are often an essential part of it.

Ultimately, Auxesis is about more than helping students succeed in their next examination. It is about helping them become curious, capable and reflective learners who understand not only what they know, but how to continue learning.

Because the real value of education is not just what students achieve today, but what they become capable of tomorrow.

What that looks like in practice

I ask questions and observe how a student approaches problems to identify misconceptions, missing foundations and difficulties applying knowledge. I adjust the pace, prompts and practice to their needs while keeping expectations high.

I check understanding by asking students to explain ideas in their own words, recognise when they apply and use them in unfamiliar situations. Their responses help me decide what to address next.

High expectations and strong support

Mistakes are useful because they show us where to look next.

I maintain high expectations while making it safe to ask questions and acknowledge uncertainty. I give constructive feedback and adjust support, from breaking a problem into smaller steps to allowing more time to work through it. Mistakes show me what to address next.

I facilitate learning and provide guidance; parents support routines and preparation; students take an active part in sessions and practise between them.
